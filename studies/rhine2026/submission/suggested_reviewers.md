# Suggested and excluded reviewers (draft of 7 November 2026, to be vetted by the author)

Nature Communications asks for "the names and contact information for qualified scientific reviewers" and lets
authors "request the exclusion of certain referees" (How to submit, read 7 November 2026). The names below are
proposals from the positioning review; the author checks each for conflicts (no co-authorship, shared funding or
institution in the last three years; no current collaboration) and adds the institutional e-mail from the
institution's page. Three to five suggestions are usual; the list offers more so that non-conflicted ones remain.

## Candidates by the expertise the paper needs

Supply-chain and production-network models of disasters (the model's closest relatives):
- Elco Koks, Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam (Institute for Environmental Studies): multiregional disaster impact models
  for Europe (MRIA), infrastructure risk, ports; Nature Communications author.
- Yasuyuki Todo, Waseda University, or Hiroyasu Inoue, University of Hyogo: firm-level supply-chain simulation of
  disasters (Nature Sustainability 2019), the firm-level counterpart of this paper's agents.
- Christian Otto, Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research: the Acclimate model, loss propagation in the global
  supply network, weather extremes.
- Christian Diem or Anton Pichler, Complexity Science Hub Vienna: firm-level production networks of a whole economy,
  partially binding Leontief production (the paper uses their criticality survey; check for conflicts if the author
  has collaborated with the Hub).
- Jasper Verschuur, Delft University of Technology: ports, trade and climate risk in global supply chains (Nature
  Communications 2022, Nature Climate Change 2023).

Rhine low water and inland-waterway economics (the empirical benchmark and the transport mechanism):
- Nils Jannsen, Kiel Institute for the World Economy: the statistical estimate of the 2018 effect on German
  industrial production that the paper uses as its benchmark (co-author of Ademmer, Jannsen and Meuchelböck 2023);
  a natural but demanding referee of the comparison.
- Olaf Jonkeren, Netherlands Institute for Transport Policy Analysis (KiM): welfare and modal-split effects of low
  water on the Rhine.
- Frederik Vinke, Delft University of Technology / Rijkswaterstaat: cascading effects of sustained low water on
  inland shipping; the vessel-loading relations the paper's loading curve rests on.
- Aris Christodoulou, European Commission Joint Research Centre (Seville): climate change and inland waterways in
  Europe, the transport-cost side.

Macroeconomics of transport and supply shocks (for the economy-wide reading):
- Vasco Carvalho, University of Cambridge: propagation of shocks in production networks (Tohoku).
- Christoph Boehm, University of Texas at Austin, or Aaron Flaaen, Federal Reserve Board: firm-level transmission of
  supply disruptions through input linkages.

## Exclusions to consider (the author's decision; give the journal a reason)

- Co-authors of the DisruptSC lineage (Stéphane Hallegatte, Julie Rozenberg, World Bank) and current IIASA
  colleagues (for example Stefan Hochrainer-Stigler): conflicts of co-authorship and institution.
- Anyone who reviewed the manuscript informally before submission.

## What to enter in the form

For each suggested reviewer: full name, institution, institutional e-mail, a one-line reason. For exclusions: name,
institution, reason. The journal is not bound by either list.
